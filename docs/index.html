Following Airbnb’s 2023–2024 identity and listing verification rollout, thousands of listings were removed nationwide. This dashboard examines the structure of the remaining market in Washington, D.C.
About This Dashboard
This dashboard presents a neighborhood-level snapshot of Washington, D.C.’s Airbnb market. The goal is to examine how supply, pricing, compliance, and host concentration appear following large-scale listing removals.

Use the map and controls to explore absolute values or relative performance compared to the citywide baseline.

Average License Compliance
Share of listings linked to a valid Washington, D.C. Short-Term Rental (STR) license. Under D.C. regulations, stays under 30 nights generally require a license unless the property, such as a hotel, qualifies for an exemption.
Median Nightly Price
The median advertised nightly rate among active listings. The median is used instead of the mean to reduce distortion from luxury outliers and better represent the typical price point.
Reviews per Month
Average number of reviews per month over the lifetime of each listing. While not a direct measure of bookings, review frequency serves as a proxy for demand and occupancy intensity.
% Multi-Property Hosts
Share of listings whose hosts operate two or more properties within the current Washington, D.C. dataset. This metric captures the presence of portfolio operators relative to single-listing hosts.
Listing Density
Active listings per 1,000 residents, using neighborhood population data from Census Reporter. This normalizes supply relative to local population size.
Total Listings
Count of active listings within each neighborhood. Relative mode expresses supply as a share of total Washington, D.C. listings, benchmarked against the average per-neighborhood share.

Market Structure & Distribution

Minimum Nights Distribution

Listings cluster at two thresholds: 1–3 night stays and 31-night minimums. The 31-night group is predominantly unlicensed, reflecting a structural shift toward medium-term rental positioning. Shorter minimums represent traditional short-term rental activity. The split highlights strategic differences in compliance and operating model.

Price Distribution

The vertical violin shows the full distribution of nightly prices. Taller and wider sections indicate greater density at those price levels, while extended upper tails reveal high-end outliers. Median markers allow direct comparison to the city baseline and expose neighborhood-level pricing spread.

Occupancy Scatter

Availability over the upcoming year serves as a proxy for occupancy. A slight downward trend suggests that listings with lower availability tend to generate more reviews. The clustering of points at lower occupancy reflects hosts who only open calendars three to six months in advance, limiting observed availability.

Lorenz Curve — Revenue Concentration

The Lorenz curve measures revenue concentration across hosts. The gray diagonal line represents perfect equality. Deviation from that line indicates concentration among higher-earning operators. The Gini coefficient quantifies this inequality (0 = equal distribution, 1 = complete concentration).

Density Bubble Plot

Listings per 1,000 residents measures short-term rental intensity relative to neighborhood population. Bubble size reflects total listings. High per-capita density with large bubble size indicates structurally concentrated short‑term rental markets.

Pareto Concentration

Neighborhoods are ranked by total listings. The cumulative curve identifies how quickly supply concentrates across the city. Neighborhoods to the left of the 80% marker account for the majority of active listings, illustrating the uneven spatial distribution of supply.
